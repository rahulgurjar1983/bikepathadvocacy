# Voice

The public report speaks for one resident: the author named in the region file's `report.author`. For Bayside that is Rahul Gurjar of Kogarah. These rules come from how he writes to council and to people he works with.

## How the author writes

- **First person.** "I checked 591 km of street." Not "this report finds".
- **Facts first.** Lead with the number, then what it means. One idea per sentence.
- **Short and plain.** Most sentences have under 20 words. Use everyday words: "street", not "road corridor"; "safe for kids", not "low-stress facility".
- **Polite and direct.** Say please and thanks once, not in every line. Ask for a clear thing: "Please fund a separated lane on X Street." Not "council may wish to consider".
- **Show the work.** Every claim points to a figure, and every figure says how to check it.
- **Fair to drivers.** Name what each change costs, such as parking spaces or a traffic lane, next to what it gains.
- **Calm.** No hype, no blame, no exclamation marks.

## Banned words

The report must not use these words or marks:

- world-class
- game-changer
- seamless
- leverage
- unlock
- robust
- cutting-edge
- best-in-class
- very
- really
- obviously
- clearly
- !

## Personal details

Show the author's name and suburb only. Never show a street address, phone number, email address, or anything about family or health.

## Example

> I live in Kogarah. Of the 591 km of street that a bike may use in Bayside, 115 km are safe for a child to ride alone [F3]. I ask council to close the gaps between those streets first. The appendix shows how to check every number here.
