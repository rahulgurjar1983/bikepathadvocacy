# Voice

The public report speaks for one resident: the author named in the region file's `report.author`. For Bayside that is Rahul Gurjar of Kogarah. These rules come from how he writes to council and to people he works with.

## How the author writes

- **First person.** "I checked 591 km of street." Not "this report finds".
- **Community outcome first.** Lead with what the selected proposal would deliver and change, then its cost, stage and council ask. Pair each gain with its local impact. Facts about street inventory and model scores follow the proposal. One idea per sentence.
- **Short and plain.** Most sentences have under 20 words. Use everyday words such as "street" and "cycling route". Plain language must still preserve the limits of a safety claim.
- **Polite and direct.** Say please and thanks once, not in every line. Ask for a clear thing: "Please fund a separated lane on X Street." Not "council may wish to consider".
- **Show the work.** Every claim points to a figure, and every figure says how to check it.
- **Fair to the community.** Name parking and traffic changes beside the gain. Show effects on walking space, trees, buses, deliveries and local access, including unknowns. Keep temporary works apart from lasting changes. Costs mean money; parking and road space are impacts.
- **Calm.** No hype, no blame, no exclamation marks.

## Safe words

Say a street meets the model's all-ages criteria. State whether its evidence is confirmed, assumed or unknown. Never guarantee child safety. Level 1 alone is "calm", since a calm street can still fail the rule.

## Banned words

The report must not use these words or marks:

- world-class
- game-changer
- seamless
- leverage
- unlock
- robust
- cutting-edge
- best-in-class
- very
- really
- obviously
- clearly
- !

## Personal details

Show the author's name and suburb only. Never show a street address, phone number, email address, or anything about family or health.

## Example

> I propose these links between homes, schools and stations. The map shows the routes and the crossings they need. The street plans show parking removed and other changes. Some widths and crossings still need checking. I ask council to fund surveys and a costed concept design. The figures and appendix show how to check each claim.

This is a voice example. Real reports use measured or modelled results from the chosen package and link each number to its figure. Never copy an example count or imply that an unfunded proposal will be delivered.
